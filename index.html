Credo

I updated my home page immediately following Rosh Hashana, September 2026 (Hebrew: Tishrei 5787), where Rosh Hashana marks the Jewish New Year that is a solemn day of prayer, introspection, and atonement that is quite the opposite of the secular New Year that is typically marked by a day of frivolity. Over the holiday I read a short book The Eternal Conversation by former UK chief rabbi Lord Rabbi Jonathan Sacks (o.b.m. 1948-2020) who was recognized by many, and not only by Jews, as being an eminent moral philosopher. In "Letter 4" on "The Pursuit of Happiness" p. 58, he wrote that it would be considered madness to deliver a eulogy where one stated that the deceased "Mr. X, what a man he was. He drove a Lamborghini, dressed in Armani, wore a Patek-Philippe, had a villa in Cap Ferrat, and a pied-a-terre in Mayfair. This was a giant. We shall not see his like again."

Therefore I felt compelled to write a short paragraph about what I feel is very important to me, and hopefully what others recognize about me:

  1. I am a proud observant God-fearing Jew.

  2. In 1983 the best life decision that I made was coming on Aliyah (English: immigrating to Eretz Israel, the Land of Israel) as a young man, with a young wife, and the older two of my then infant children. The decision was beneficial both spiritually and professionally. I see that "Hashem watches over the Land, where the eyes of Hashem are always upon it, from the beginning of the year until the end of the year" (Deut. 11:12). (The Hebrew word Hashem, in English literally the "Name", is the term that most Jews traditionally have used from time immemorial when referring to the ineffable name of God).

  3. During the 15 years that I served in an IDF reserve combat unit in a command position, I grew both spiritually and personally. And I must add, emphatically to the contrary of the Hareidi 9 (i.e. the so-called "ultra orthodox") view, that joining the IDF enhances one's spiritual growth.

  4. In a number of instances I had the opportunity to follow the fundamental "marching orders" that Hashem gave to Avraham Avinu (English: our forefather Abraham), namely "to keep the ways of Hashem, to execute righteousness and justice" (Gen. 18:19), and to teach my children accordingly.
